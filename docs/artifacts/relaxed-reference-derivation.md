# Relaxed reference costs: derivation and policy families

The two reference costs are given in the calibration table of the Simulation chapter (right block). 
## Derivation (former Appendix A)

<!-- Definitions and numbers: spec §4.13 item 2 (LB assumptions (i) to (vi), state = information set; correction 2: the controller's effective state is the exact demand, not the 10-UE bucket) and §4.32 item 8 (LB_strict / LB_matched under measured physics; LB_matched = fitted margins scaled by one common factor c ≥ 1 until held-out risk ≤ p, "a feasible but not necessarily optimal policy"); the four numbers are those of the Simulation chapter's Table 2 (§4.32 item 5). The two reference costs of the calibration table relax the controller's problem inside one policy family; neither bounds all causal policies. Both are computed on the held-out traces of grouped splits 0 to 2 as the sum of two parts. <!-- §4.13 item 2 assumption (vi); §4.32 item 5 --> The structural part is the cost of a clairvoyant that knows the trace, holds continuous units, releases them for free and places each layer's order exactly one lead ahead of the demand it serves, so that lead time, integer units, teardown billing and release rules cost nothing. <!-- §4.13 item 2 assumptions (i), (vi) --> The margin part prices, per layer, the expected margin $m_l(s)$ that a per-state policy must hold so that the probability that the demand rise over the layer's horizon exceeds the margin is at most ε, with margins fitted on the calibration traces of the split and risk and margin re-measured on the held-out traces; the per-layer constraint replaces the controller's any-layer per-minute constraint, which is itself a relaxation. <!-- §4.13 item 2 assumptions (ii), (iii), (v) -->

LB_strict. The margin policy sees the exact demand state and is used as fitted. It drops the uncertainty and the lead-time constraint. Its held-out per-layer risk exceeds the constraint (1.8 to 2.5 % at the 2 % target, 0.5 to 0.75 % at the 0.5 % target), so it is not feasible at the target and is a floor for its family only. <!-- §4.32 item 8 -->

LB_matched. The margin policy keeps the controller's information set, the current demand state, and its fitted margins are scaled by one common factor c ≥ 1 until the held-out risk meets the target. It drops the lead-time constraint and the unit granularity but keeps the uncertainty the controller faces. As a feasible but not optimal member of the family, it is an upper estimate of the family's optimum, which lies between LB_strict and LB_matched. <!-- §4.32 item 8 -->

Policy families. Both references fix the margin as a deterministic function of the per-state information set, fit it on calibration traces rather than optimising it on the evaluation traces, and relax the any-layer constraint to per-layer constraints; a policy that uses demand history beyond the current state, or a randomised policy, lies outside the family. <!-- §4.13 item 2 assumptions (iii), (iv), (v) --> The gaps in that table therefore price integer units, teardown billing and conservative release against a clairvoyant structure of the same family. <!-- §4.32 item 5: 1292.5 vs 1230.6 / 1253.6; 1346.8 vs 1294.8 / 1343.4 -->
